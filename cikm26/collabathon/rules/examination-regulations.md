# General Examination Regulations of the University of Wernstadt

This text was invented for the UniAgent hackathon "The University of the Future" (8 November 2026). The University of Wernstadt does not exist, and these regulations are not the regulations of any real university. They decide the requests of the hackathon's teaching scenario and nothing else.

## § 1 Scope and definitions

(1) These regulations apply to all Bachelor's and Master's programmes of the University of Wernstadt.

(2) Students send requests under these regulations to the examination office (pruefungsamt@uni-wernstadt.de) in text form. A request counts as received on the day it reaches the examination office. Documents that a request relies on must be attached to it.

(3) Working days are Monday to Friday, except public holidays in the state of Hessen. Calendar days are all days of the week. Example: after an examination on Wednesday, 13 October 2027, the third working day is Monday, 18 October 2027.

(4) A deadline that runs "until the seventh calendar day before the examination date" includes that day. Example: for an examination on Friday, 15 October 2027, the seventh calendar day before it is Friday, 8 October 2027.

(5) A Bachelor's programme comprises 180 credits (ECTS), a Master's programme 120 credits.

(6) The examination office decides on every request in writing.

## § 2 Registration for written examinations

(1) Students register for each written examination in the examination portal. The registration period opens on the 28th calendar day before the examination date and closes on the 14th calendar day before it. Example: for an examination on Friday, 15 October 2027, the registration period runs from Friday, 17 September 2027 to Friday, 1 October 2027.

(2) After the registration period has closed, a student may request late registration until the seventh calendar day before the examination date. Requests received later are denied.

(3) Late registration is granted only if a reason beyond the student's control prevented registration on every day of the registration period, and the student shows this reason with a document. Such reasons are an illness (medical certificate or hospital letter) and a technical fault of the student's portal account (written confirmation by the IT service). Forgetting the deadline, travel, and employment are not such reasons.

## § 3 Withdrawal from written examinations

(1) A student may withdraw from a registered written examination without giving reasons until the seventh calendar day before the examination date.

(2) After that day, a student may withdraw only for a reason beyond the student's control that the student shows with a document. A lack of preparation, a move, and employment are not such reasons. Illness on the examination date is governed by § 4.

(3) Paragraph 1 does not apply to an examination in the third attempt (§ 5). A student may withdraw from a third attempt only under paragraph 2.

## § 4 Illness on the examination date

(1) A student who misses a registered examination because of illness must notify the examination office and submit a medical certificate no later than on the third working day after the examination date.

(2) The certificate must be issued by a physician who examined the student no later than on the examination date, and it must state that the student was unfit to take examinations on the examination date.

(3) If the examination office accepts the illness, the examination counts as not taken and the attempt does not count. Otherwise the examination is graded "failed" (5.0).

## § 5 Repeat examinations

(1) A failed module examination may be repeated twice, so a student has at most three attempts at each module examination.

(2) An attempt counts if the examination was graded "failed", including an absence without an accepted reason. An examination cancelled under § 4(3) and a withdrawal under § 3 do not count as attempts.

(3) A passed examination cannot be repeated, not even to improve the grade.

(4) Registration for the third attempt requires a counselling interview with the student advisor of the programme. The request must include the advisor's written confirmation of the interview.

(5) A student who fails the third attempt has finally failed the module examination.

## § 6 Bachelor's and Master's theses

(1) The processing time is 12 weeks for a Bachelor's thesis and 24 weeks for a Master's thesis. The confirmation of the thesis registration states the submission deadline.

(2) On request, the examination office extends the submission deadline if a reason beyond the student's control prevents work on the thesis and the student shows this reason with a document. Such reasons are an illness (medical certificate) and a delay that the supervisor confirms in writing, for example missing access to equipment or data. Workload from employment and other examinations are not such reasons.

(3) The deadline is extended by the period requested, but by no more than four weeks for a Bachelor's thesis and six weeks for a Master's thesis.

(4) The request must reach the examination office before the submission deadline. A submission deadline can be extended only once.

(5) If the extended deadline falls on a Saturday, a Sunday, or a public holiday in Hessen, it moves to the next working day.

## § 7 Recognition of credits from stays abroad

(1) Credits earned at a partner university during a stay abroad are recognized for the modules listed in a learning agreement that the examination office approved before the stay began. The approved learning agreement establishes that these modules are comparable to the modules they replace. Modules that the learning agreement does not list are not recognized, and without a learning agreement approved in time no credits are recognized.

(2) Only passed modules are recognized. Whether a module was passed follows from the grading scale stated on the transcript of records of the partner university.

(3) A recognized module counts with the credits earned at the partner university, but with no more credits than the module it replaces under the learning agreement.

(4) Recognized credits may not exceed half of the credits of the programme in total, that is 90 credits in a Bachelor's programme and 60 credits in a Master's programme. Credits recognized earlier under any regulation count toward this limit.

(5) The request must include the approved learning agreement and the transcript of records. The examination office grants the request if at least one credit is recognized, and it states the number of recognized credits.

## § 8 Entry into force

These regulations enter into force on 1 October 2026. They apply to every request received from that day on.
